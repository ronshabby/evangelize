Hebrews Letter | September 30, 2026

Faith for My Need or Faith for God's Purpose?

Hebrews 11 speaks about the faith of the forefathers. Abel, Enoch, Noah, Abraham, Moses, and many others obtained a good testimony through faith. God testified of them that they well-pleased Him. In Hebrews 12, they become that great "cloud of witnesses".

But the faith Hebrews 11 talks about is not just initial faith. Jesus is the author and perfecter of faith. Our faith should therefore grow and come to perfection.

Faith for My Need?

When we think of faith, we very often think first of our personal needs: "I need something, so I must believe." I worry about whether I will pass the exam, so I believe that the Lord will help me. I have a job interview or a problem and need faith for it. This is not wrong. Of course we can and should experience the Lord in our personal lives.

But Hebrews 11 is about more than just our personal needs. It is about God wanting to fulfill His plan and purpose on this earth.

Faith for God's Purpose

Let's look at the examples in Hebrews 11.

Abel did not use faith for his own cause, but for the worship of God. He knew: God wants a lamb from me. He offered this sacrifice to God and was even persecuted and killed for it.

Enoch walked with God. He did God's will and experienced faith in doing so.

Noah received instructions from God to build an ark. He did not yet see what was coming, but he believed God and built the ark. That was for God's plan and purpose.

Very often we use faith for our personal needs, but very little for God's needs. We believe: "Lord, help me to do well." But what about God's purpose? What does God want to do today?

Hebrews 11:24-26

"²⁴ By faith Moses, when he became great, refused to be called the son of Pharaoh’s daughter, ²⁵ choosing rather to be mistreated with the people of God than to enjoy the passing pleasures of sin, ²⁶ esteeming the reproach of Christ greater riches than the treasures of Egypt; for he looked away to the reward."

What Has First Priority?

This becomes especially clear with Moses. From a human perspective, Moses had the best conditions for a career. He grew up in Pharaoh's house and had an excellent education. Perhaps he could have even become Pharaoh.

Yet Moses did not use his faith to achieve his next career step. He preferred to be mistreated with the people of God because God had called him to a service. What has first priority in our hearts? Our career? Our education? We are here for God's kingdom. For that, we need faith.

Living Faith Instead of Religion

How did Abel, Noah, and the others even know what God wanted? It sprang from their fellowship with the Lord. This reveals the secret: faith is a living relationship with the living God.

To believe means not only to believe in God, but also to believe God—to believe His sayings, to believe His plan, and to act accordingly. And that includes being willing to give our self into death.

Therefore let us pray: Lord, what do You want to do today? What do You want to have from me? And how can I cooperate with You through faith so that Your purpose goes forward?

Source: Bibeltreff Stuttgart (translated from German).
